## 1. Rules every piece of marketing must follow

These keep the plan free of false claims. Check every ad, post, page and script against them.

1. **Only claim what is true on the day it is published.** If a feature isn't shipped and tested, don't mention it.
2. **Never say** "BIR-ready", "BIR-accredited", "BIR-approved" or "official receipt". POS-034 prints order slips marked "Not an official receipt". The shop keeps issuing its own BIR invoices.
3. **"Pay once" means a one-time license**, not ownership of the software. Say "pay once, use it for good", never "own it". Yearly maintenance is optional and must be shown next to the price.
4. **"Works without internet" needs its footnote:** internet is needed only to activate, back up and update, and those wait until the shop is back online.
5. **No invented numbers.** No market statistics, savings figures or customer counts unless there's a source or your own real data behind them.
6. **No invented or edited testimonials.** Real customers only, with written permission for name, photo and quote.
7. **Don't name or compare competitors.** Describe the problem ("cloud POS stops when the internet drops"), not a brand.
8. **Legal and tax statements** only in the form a tax practitioner has confirmed (design §12).
9. **Prices on the website include every mandatory cost.** The one-time license, what setup costs, and that maintenance is optional.
10. **Lead forms** state what's collected and why, and ask for consent (Data Privacy Act, RA 10173).

## 2. Positioning

**Who it's for:** owners of small Philippine shops (restaurants and carinderias, hardware stores,
auto parts and service shops, tire and mags shops, car washes) who are price-sensitive, have patchy
internet and aren't technical.

**The problem:** monthly-fee cloud POS apps stop when the internet drops. Stock counts drift.
Utang lives in a notebook.

**Promise (headline):** **"Pay once. Works offline. Stock that adds up."**

**Three proof points, used everywhere:**
1. **One-time license.** No monthly fee; yearly maintenance is optional.
2. **Works offline.** Selling, stock, utang and reports run on the tablet.
3. **Stock can't go below zero.** Low-stock alerts before you run out.

**Supporting points:** utang list with balances and payment slips; quotations that turn into sales
at the quoted price; staff PINs with who-did-what on every sale; void-never-delete; encrypted
nightly backup and restore onto a new tablet.

## 3. Brand

- **kennali.com**, the parent brand: "Simple apps for small businesses. Pay once." Every future app keeps the one-time-price, works-offline promise, so each app vouches for the next.
- **POS-034**, the product. Always written with the hyphen. The product site is `pos-034.kennali.com`.
- **Voice:** plain, friendly English with light Taglish. Short sentences. Talk about the shop's day, not technology.
- **Visuals:** real photos of the tablet and printer in real shops; real screenshots. No stock photos of people pretending to be customers.

---

## 4. Offer

Set the prices after the pilots (§6). Use these packages:

| Package | Includes |
|---|---|
| **License** | 1 tablet, 1 year of updates + support |
| **Setup** | On-site install, product import from their list, staff training, printer pairing |
| **Bundle** | License + setup + a tested tablet + printer, ready to sell |
| **Maintenance renewal** | Optional, yearly: updates + support. The app keeps working without it |

**Pricing checklist before publishing prices:**
- Cost of the tablet and printer (bundle) plus margin.
- Your hours for setup, travel included.
- Support time per shop per year (measured on the pilots).
- Test the price with the pilots and 5–10 prospects before it goes on the website.

## 5. Channels (in order: cheapest first)

1. **Walk-in visits in your city.** Carry a tablet with the demo business matching the shop in front of you. Keep the demo to 3 minutes: a sale, a utang entry, a low-stock alert. Leave a printed card with `pos-034.kennali.com` and your Messenger link.
2. **Facebook page + Messenger.** The main contact button on every page, ad and card.
3. **Short videos** (Facebook Reels, TikTok): one problem per video, filmed on the real app. Ideas:
   - "Internet down at lunch rush? Keep selling."
   - "Who owes you? Utang list in two taps."
   - "The last tire can't be sold twice."
   - "Quote today, sell next week at the same price."
4. **Local Facebook groups** (city business and buy-and-sell groups, hardware and automotive groups). Post useful content and demo videos, follow each group's rules, no spam.
5. **Referrals.** Reward a shop that refers a paying customer, e.g. a free maintenance year. State the reward publicly.
6. **Partners.** Tablet and printer sellers, bookkeepers serving small shops, distributors who visit many stores. Agree any commission in writing.
7. **Paid Facebook ads.** Only after the pilots produce real videos and testimonials. Target by city and business interest; start small and measure cost per demo booked.

## 6. Launch sequence

1. **Pilots:** 3–4 shops, **one per industry** (restaurant, hardware, auto/tire, car wash), at a discount. In return: weekly feedback for a month, a short video testimonial, and written permission to use their name. You install them all yourself.
2. **Proof:** each pilot becomes an industry page on `pos-034.kennali.com` with their real photos, real screenshots and their quote.
3. **Local push:** walk-ins plus the Facebook page in your city until installs come by referral.
4. **Expand:** partners, then ads, in nearby cities.

---

## 7. Website (`pos-034.kennali.com`)

Mobile-first (owners arrive from Facebook on a phone).

1. **Hero:** headline + the three proof points; buttons **Try the demo** and **Message us**.
2. **Pick your business:** Restaurant · Hardware · Auto & Tires · Car Wash. Each opens its industry page and a demo preloaded with that business.
3. **Features:** only shipped ones (rule 1).
4. **How it works:** we set it up → practice mode → go live.
5. **Pricing:** the §4 packages with real prices, maintenance marked optional, the internet footnote (rule 4).
6. **FAQ:**
   - Do I need internet?
   - What tablet and printer do I need?
   - What if the tablet breaks?
   - Does it replace my BIR invoices? (No.)
   - What if I stop paying maintenance?
   - Who can see my sales? (Only you; backups are encrypted with your recovery code.)
7. **Contact:** Messenger, Viber, phone.
8. **Footer:** registered business name, address, EULA, privacy notice.

## 8. Before the first peso of marketing (vendor side)

- Register the business (DTI for a sole proprietorship), plus a mayor's permit and BIR registration, so you can issue **your own invoices for license sales**.
- EULA and privacy notice published (they cover the encrypted backups and heartbeat you receive).
- Facebook page created under the kennali brand; Messenger replies set up.
- Demo businesses and web demo tested (design §9).

---

## 9. What to measure

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| Lead source (walk-in, FB, video, group, referral, partner, ad) | Where to spend time |
| Demos given → installs | Is the demo convincing? |
| Days from first contact to install | Where installs stall |
| Support hours per shop, first 3 months | Real cost for pricing |
| Maintenance renewal rate after year 1 | Long-term income |
| Referrals per installed shop | Word of mouth working? |

Review monthly. Drop any channel that brings no installs after two honest months.
